# Public-Facing Rates Page — Strategy & Design Brief

**Prepared for:** Creative Director
**From:** Ry · July 2026
**Status:** Pre-design, direction approved

> **The decision:** We are building a **new, public, evergreen rates page** for
> wolfstrategyllc.com traffic. It is a companion to — not a replacement for — the existing
> quarter-stamped rates page we share with active prospects by direct link. This brief
> explains the strategy so your design decisions follow from it.

---

## 1. Where this page sits in the funnel

Today, pricing appears nowhere on the public site. Our detailed rates page
(`intake.wolfstrategyllc.com/rates_2026Q3`) is unindexed and reaches people only after a
conversation has started — a true middle-of-funnel artifact.

The new page changes that posture deliberately. The moment rates are linked from the
homepage or the upcoming "Business Applications built" page, the audience is no longer warm
prospects — it includes **cold first-visit traffic, competitors, and tire-kickers**. Design
for a reader who knows almost nothing about us yet. That's the single biggest difference
from the Q3 page, and most of the directives below follow from it.

The expected path: **Applications page (proof of work) → rates page → book a call.** The
rates page is the bridge between "they seem credible" and "let's talk." Its one job is to
get a good-fit visitor to the booking CTA feeling informed, and a bad-fit visitor to
self-select out before they take a calendar slot.

## 2. Why we're publishing rates at all

- **It proves the brand promise.** Our voice is anti-hype, engineering-doc,
  transparent-math. A firm that talks that way but hides pricing behind a contact form
  reads as off-brand. Public rates are the credibility receipt for the positioning — the
  page should *feel* like that transparency, not just state it.
- **Self-qualification beats lead volume.** Call time is the scarcest resource. Visible
  rates filter out mismatched budgets before they book. Fewer, better calls is the success
  metric — a drop in raw inquiries is expected and fine.
- **Almost no competing consultancy does this.** Published rates are a differentiator, and
  they pre-anchor discovery calls at "which tier fits" instead of "can I afford you."

## 3. Design directives

**Evergreen, not quarter-stamped.** No quarter or year in the URL, headline, or framing. A
quiet **"rates last reviewed: [date]"** line is welcome — it signals maintenance without
dating the page. The Q3-style deck remains our dated, detailed leave-behind.

**Less granular than the internal rates page.** Tiers and ranges, not the full rate ladder.
The public page anchors and qualifies; the direct-link page itemizes. Don't carry the Q3
page's full pricing detail forward.

**Context before numbers.** A warm prospect can open on figures; a cold visitor can't. Give
a short value-framing section — who this is for, what an engagement looks like — **before**
the first price appears. Keep it tight; this is framing, not a hero-heavy landing page.

**"Standard engagements" framing.** Present published rates as standard engagements, with
an explicit note that **larger or unusual scopes are custom-quoted**. This preserves
negotiating room on big deals without undermining the transparency posture.

**Voice: engineering doc, not brochure.** Punchy, truthful, math-shown. No superlatives,
no "world-class." Where a number has a rationale, show the rationale — that's the house
style and the trust mechanism.

**Brand system carries over.** Navy `#000B29` ground, coral `#F95954` strictly rationed
(CTAs and key accents only), the established type and 4px-radius card system from the
existing rates page. This page should look like a sibling of the Q3 page, one step more
public.

**Indexed and shareable.** Unlike the Q3 page, this one is **meant to be found** — no
noindex. Assume it gets linked, screenshotted, and read out of context; every section
should stand alone reasonably well.

**One CTA: book a call.** The page resolves to the existing calendar-booking CTA. No
competing conversions — no newsletter, no contact form fork. Rates → confidence → call.

## 5. Known trade-offs (accepted)

- **Competitors see our rates.** Accepted — largely irrelevant at our scale, and the
  differentiation is worth more.
- **Some negotiating flexibility is lost.** Mitigated by the "standard engagements /
  custom above threshold" framing above.
- **Publishing is a maintenance commitment.** A public rates page that drifts stale is
  worse than none; the "last reviewed" line makes the commitment visible.

## 6. Open questions — don't design around these yet

- **Where the page lives is not decided.** There's an open consolidation question across
  our web properties (Wix site plus the GitHub-hosted pages). Design the page; placement
  and URL get settled separately.
- **How the "Business Applications built" page hands off to this one** — link placement,
  shared visual language, whether rates get teased there. To be worked out once both pages
  have a first draft.
- **Homepage navigation treatment** — whether "Pricing" earns a top-nav slot or is reached
  only via the Applications page. Leaning toward deciding after we see engagement on the
  Applications page.
