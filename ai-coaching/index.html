The curriculum · Taught, not toured

Skills that compound, from session one

Learning AI is unlike any technology you’ve had to learn before — it’s run-before-you-walk, and it’s more fun. Underneath the fun is a real curriculum.

  • The mentality shift. Stop thinking, then writing. With AI as a colleague, thinking and writing happen at once — that shift is the foundation everything else builds on.
  • Never start from a blank page again. Workflows that turn a spoken description of what you want into drafts, documents, and plans.
  • Train it just as you would a human. Memory, projects, and preferences — so your AI shows up already knowing who you are and how you like to work.
  • Prompting that transfers understanding. Good prompting isn’t magic words — it’s giving AI enough context to genuinely help. Audience, context, and inviting the AI to ask questions first.
  • Verify like a professional. AI creates the draft; you remain responsible for the result. The checking habits that make AI output safe to sign.
  • Know what not to paste. Confidentiality judgment built around your industry and your employer’s AI policy. When in doubt, leave it out.
Safe by design

Built for confidential and regulated work

Plenty of students work in law, healthcare, finance, education, or HR. Safe AI use isn’t a disclaimer here — it’s part of the curriculum, and it’s how the whole program is run.

  • Advice fitted to your workplace’s AI policy — what you can use, and what stays out
  • Industry-specific confidentiality rules set up inside your own AI from day one
  • A student ID stands in for your name on everything sent to you
  • Sessions are strictly one-on-one — your materials are never mixed with anyone else’s
