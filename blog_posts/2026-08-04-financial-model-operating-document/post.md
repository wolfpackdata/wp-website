Most of the business leaders I talk to hold the same idea about what a financial
model is for. It's for the accountant.
It's for the raise. And it isn't worth much time until the business is far
enough along to make it correct.

That's the wrong job for it.

A financial model is not an accounting document. It's an operating document.

The correctness isn't the part that produces the value. The exercise of building
it is. Building it forces the crucial details of the business onto paper:
pricing, cost lines, headcount, timing, the order things have to happen in, and
the assumptions holding all of it up. It also surfaces the golden paths, the
places where revenue or profit is high relative to the work and the expense that
generate them. Those paths are hard to see from inside the business. They're
obvious from inside the math.

None of that is permission to present guesses as facts. Numbers you hand an
investor or a lender still have to be defensible, and a straw model is wrong in
places by design.

## Not Ready Is the Symptom

When I offer to help build one, the answer is consistent. They're not ready.
They don't know which assumptions to put in yet. They want to develop the
product further first.

What I see in the same conversation is someone stressed and stuck. Decision
paralysis is the normal state for a founder with no model to look at, because
every choice has to be re-derived from nothing every time it comes up.

## What It Actually Costs

Two to three days builds a straw model: one P&L with your real cost lines, your
headcount, and honest guesses where you don't know yet. Somewhere
between 30 and 90 minutes a week keeps the whole picture loaded in your head,
and one day at month-end close keeps it real. That's the entire bill.

What comes back is a return figure on your own hours, by project, by product,
and by sub-product. You can't produce that figure in your head. You'll estimate
it, and you'll be wrong in the direction of whatever you already wanted to work
on. There are roughly 2,000 workable hours in a year, so
you have to pick.

The model doesn't make the decision. It shows infeasibility with math, and it
leaves the actual choice with you.

## Where to Start

Block the two to three days. Then put the weekly hour on the calendar and keep
it. That's the whole practice.
